    What is a PID?

    We want the ball to sit still at one point of the beam. The controller measures where the ball is about 30 times a second, works out the difference from the target (the error) and decides how far to tilt the beam. That decision is the sum of three ideas:

    P · the present

    Tilts more the further away the ball is. It is the main push, but on its own it makes the ball swing back and forth like a pendulum.

    I · the past

    Adds up the error as it piles up. If the ball stops close to the target but never reaches it, the I pushes a little harder each time until it does.

    D · the future

    Looks at how fast the ball is moving and brakes it before it arrives, like the shock absorber of a car.

    Try this:

    1. Press «P only»: the ball never stops going back and forth.
    2. Raise the D bit by bit until the ball stops.
    3. See whether it settles a few millimetres short of the target. Raise the I a little and watch it correct that.
    4. Change the target and try to beat the record time.

    Competition: on the «Competition» tab the program deals out a random target and random settings, almost always bad ones, and starts the clock. You have to move P, I and D until the ball sits still on the target for three seconds in a row. Your time goes on the leaderboard.

    PIDs are everywhere: in the cruise control of a car, in drones, in an oven, in the climate of a greenhouse or in a water treatment plant.
